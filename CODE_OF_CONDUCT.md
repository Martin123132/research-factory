# Code of conduct

Research Factory welcomes contributors regardless of credentials, affiliation,
experience, disability, identity or background. Evidence is judged against the
published contract; people are treated with patience and respect.

## Expected conduct

- Critique claims, methods, measurements and assumptions rather than people.
- Record uncertainty, failed attempts and conflicting evidence honestly.
- Give credit accurately and do not claim another person's work as your own.
- Respect blind evaluation, private identity records, holdouts and embargoes.
- Accept that a failed gate is a request for better evidence, not a judgment of
  intelligence or worth.
- Help newcomers reproduce the standard without lowering the standard itself.

Disagreement is not misconduct. Firm technical criticism, rejection of an
unsupported claim and requests for reproducible evidence are legitimate when
expressed without harassment, threats, humiliation or discrimination.

## Unacceptable conduct

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, doxxing, impersonation,
credential theft, deliberate benchmark leakage, falsified evidence, retaliation
and sustained disruption are not accepted. Do not pressure anyone to disclose
confidential, patent-sensitive or personally identifying material.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not place sensitive reports in a public issue. Use GitHub's private security
reporting channel when available, or contact the repository owner privately
through their GitHub profile. Include the smallest amount of evidence needed to
understand the incident.

Maintainers may warn a participant, limit access, remove content or exclude a
participant when necessary to protect people or the evidence system. Reports
will be handled as privately as practical, conflicts of interest should be
declared, and enforcement decisions should state the violated rule without
unnecessarily exposing a reporter.

This code applies in repository spaces and in project-related communication.
