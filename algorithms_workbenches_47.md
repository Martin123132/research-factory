# Practical benchmarkable workbenches — 47

I fixed one common promotion rule across all 47: freeze the benchmark version, preserve a public development set plus hidden holdouts, run an independent correctness verifier, compare against a pinned reproducible baseline, and promote only a genuine Pareto improvement—not a headline metric bought with absurd compute, memory, energy or cost.

## Compression, storage and representation — 12

1. **General-purpose lossless compression.** Gate: SHA-256-identical reconstruction on every public and hidden file; measure compressed bytes, encode/decode throughput, peak RAM and joules. Promote only a smaller/faster Pareto point against pinned `zstd`, `xz` and other verified anchors. Economic guardrail: total CPU, energy and storage cost per retained TB must fall, with a deployable decoder. Source: [Silesia Corpus](https://data-compression.info/Corpora/SilesiaCorpus/index.html).

2. **Large-text and knowledge-archive compression.** Gate: exact `enwik9` restoration, with compressor/decompressor size, runtime and memory accounted exactly under the challenge rules. Economic guardrail: compute cost must be justified by archive lifetime and retrieval frequency; prohibit “tiny archive, week-long decoder” claims from being presented as general deployment wins. Source: [Hutter Prize](https://prize.hutter1.net/).

3. **Genomic alignment compression.** Gate: CRAM-conformant files whose decoded records, tags, qualities and reference identifiers match the canonical input; verify indexed random access as well as full decoding. Measure bytes/read, encoding, decoding, memory and range-read latency against CRAM 3.1 profiles. Economic guardrail: storage and egress savings must exceed added CPU and reference-management cost. Source: [HTSlib CRAM benchmarks](https://www.htslib.org/benchmarks/CRAM.html).

4. **Error-bounded scientific-array compression.** Gate: every value respects the declared absolute/relative error bound; additionally freeze domain-specific quantities of interest so a compressor cannot preserve PSNR while corrupting the scientific result. Measure ratio, throughput, RAM, PSNR/NRMSE and downstream-error deltas. Economic guardrail: saved storage/I/O time must exceed compression compute and any scientifically acceptable loss. Source: [SDRBench](https://sdrbench.github.io/).

5. **Multispectral and hyperspectral spacecraft-image compression.** Gate: conformance to lossless or declared near-lossless bounds, bit-accurate decoding, worst-case memory and deterministic operation; measure bits/pixel, samples/s, latency and energy. Economic guardrail: downlink savings must exceed onboard power, FPGA area and qualification cost; no unbounded memory or unpredictable worst case. Source: [CCSDS 123.0-B-2](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/123x0b2e1c3_tc2101.pdf).

6. **Still-image rate–distortion compression.** Gate: fixed bitrates on public and hidden CLIC images, valid decoding, objective scores plus the benchmark’s blinded preference procedure, decoder-size cap, latency, RAM and energy. Economic guardrail: report CDN/storage savings per million images net of encode/decode cost; prohibit server-only decoders masquerading as mobile-ready improvements. Source: [CLIC](https://clic2025.compression.cc/).

7. **Video codec efficiency.** Gate: follow the current AOM common test conditions exactly; report BD-rate across required quality metrics, bitstream validity, encode/decode time, peak RAM and energy on every sequence. Economic guardrail: calculate total encode, delivery and playback cost per viewing hour; a gain fails if target devices cannot decode within their power/latency envelope. Source: [AOM AV2 Common Test Conditions v9](https://aomedia.org/docs/CWG-G082_AV2_CTC_v9.pdf).

8. **3D point-cloud compression.** Gate: use MPEG G-PCC common test conditions for lossless and lossy geometry/attribute tracks; verify geometry validity and report bitrate, geometry/color distortion, runtime and memory. Economic guardrail: network/storage savings must exceed edge decode energy and latency, with separate profiles for maps, LiDAR and immersive media. Source: [MPEG-I G-PCC](https://www.mpeg.org/standards/MPEG-I/9/).

9. **Lossless audio compression.** Gate: sample-for-sample, channel-for-channel PCM recovery across the EBU SQAM material; measure total bytes, streaming startup, seek correctness, encode/decode throughput, RAM and energy against FLAC anchors. Economic guardrail: storage/bandwidth savings must exceed compute cost and retain real-time decoding on inexpensive devices. Source: [EBU SQAM lossless test material](https://qc.ebu.io/testmaterials/523/).

10. **Streaming telemetry compression.** Gate: CCSDS-conformant, bit-identical round trip across frozen integer telemetry distributions; include packet-boundary recovery, worst-case expansion, corruption containment, cycles/sample, RAM and latency. Economic guardrail: radio time saved must exceed onboard power and implementation risk; worst-case expansion must fit the link budget. Source: [CCSDS lossless-data-compression publications](https://ccsds.org/searchpubs/).

11. **Tiny neural-model compression.** Gate: retain every MLPerf Tiny task’s prescribed accuracy while measuring executable/model bytes, latency, peak RAM and optional energy on declared hardware; hidden inputs test generalization. Economic guardrail: reduce flash/RAM BOM or energy per inference without shifting an uneconomic retraining or cloud cost elsewhere. Source: [MLPerf Tiny](https://mlcommons.org/benchmarks/inference-tiny/).

12. **Storage deduplication and content-defined chunking.** Gate: replay frozen SNIA traces, reconstruct every byte exactly, and test crash recovery; measure physical bytes, read/write latency, CPU, RAM, metadata overhead and write amplification. Economic guardrail: report cost per usable protected TB and restore SLA; capacity savings do not count if recovery or sustained writes become commercially unacceptable. Source: [SNIA IOTTA trace repository](https://www.snia.org/educational-library/iotta-repository-2019).

## Routing, scheduling, packing and logistics — 14

13. **Travelling-salesperson route kernel.** Gate: verifier confirms one valid tour and exact TSPLIB distance; compare tour length, optimality gap, time, RAM and energy under fixed budgets. Any claimed optimum requires a certificate, not merely the best observed tour. Economic guardrail: compute cost per route must be smaller than the saved travel value. Source: [TSPLIB](https://comopt.ifi.uni-heidelberg.de/software/TSPLIB95/index.html).

14. **Capacitated fleet routing.** Gate: all customers served once, depot and vehicle-capacity constraints satisfied, and cost recalculated by the canonical CVRPLIB convention; report fleet size, distance, gap and compute. Economic guardrail: fuel/labour savings must exceed planning cost, and an algorithm must finish inside the real dispatch window. Source: [CVRPLIB](https://galgos.inf.puc-rio.br/cvrplib/).

15. **Delivery routing with time windows.** Gate: independently verify capacity, service times, arrival windows and SINTEF’s hierarchical vehicle-count/distance objective; report feasibility rate, fleet, distance and compute across Solomon and Homberger instances. Economic guardrail: value lateness and vehicles explicitly, and reject solutions too slow for daily replanning. Source: [SINTEF VRPTW](https://www.sintef.no/projectweb/top/vrptw/).

16. **Pickup-and-delivery routing with time windows.** Gate: every request’s pickup precedes its matched delivery, vehicle loads remain legal and all time windows hold; use SINTEF’s checker and report vehicles, distance, gap and runtime. Economic guardrail: planning compute plus deadheading must be less than saved fleet/labour cost. Source: [SINTEF Li & Lim PDPTW](https://www.sintef.no/projectweb/top/pdptw/).

17. **Driver-aware last-mile route sequencing.** Gate: train only on the published split and evaluate blindly on held-out Amazon routes; measure official sequence score, route time and compute, with package and time-window feasibility checked separately. Economic guardrail: estimate minutes, miles and cloud cost per route; improvements must survive multiple cities rather than one depot. Source: [Amazon Last Mile Challenge dataset](https://registry.opendata.aws/amazon-last-mile-challenges/).

18. **Dynamic ride-pooling and taxi dispatch.** Gate: replay frozen NYC TLC days chronologically so future requests cannot leak backward; verify capacity and pickup/drop-off order, then measure wait time, detour, rejected trips, empty kilometres, p95 service and compute. Economic guardrail: net revenue, driver utilization and passenger cost must improve after dispatch compute and repositioning. Source: [NYC TLC trip records](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page).

19. **One-dimensional bin packing and cutting stock.** Gate: every item appears exactly once, no bin exceeds capacity, and bin count/waste is recalculated by an independent verifier; report gap and solve time across BPPLIB. Economic guardrail: material/container savings must exceed solver time and operational complexity. Source: [University of Bologna BPPLIB](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/683159).

20. **Two-dimensional sheet cutting and nesting.** Gate: validate dimensions, rotations, non-overlap, stock boundaries, kerf and any guillotine restrictions; measure sheets used, waste area, cut length and compute on standardized instances. Economic guardrail: material and machine-time savings must exceed planning and additional toolpath/setup cost. Source: [PackLib²](https://www.ibr.cs.tu-bs.de/alg/packlib/instances.shtml).

21. **Three-dimensional pallet/container loading.** Gate: independently verify orientation rules, containment, non-overlap, weight, support, stacking and centre-of-mass constraints; measure containers, volume utilization, damage-risk proxies and runtime. Economic guardrail: freight savings must exceed planning and handling complexity; physically unstable arrangements are invalid regardless of volume. Source: [PackLib² multidimensional library](https://www.ibr.cs.tu-bs.de/alg/packlib/index.shtml).

22. **Job-shop manufacturing scheduling.** Gate: each operation uses its required machine once, precedence holds and machine intervals never overlap; independently recalculate makespan and gap across JSPLib. Economic guardrail: saved machine/labour time must exceed scheduling compute and rescheduling disruption; include a fixed short-response track. Source: [JSPLib](https://scheduleopt.github.io/benchmarks/jsplib/).

23. **Flow-shop/assembly-line sequencing.** Gate: preserve each job’s machine order and non-overlap, then verify makespan on the full Taillard sets; report gap, runtime, RAM and schedule stability. Economic guardrail: increased throughput must exceed planning and changeover cost; publish the value of a one-percent makespan reduction for the declared line. Source: [Taillard scheduling instances](https://mistic.iict-heig-vd.ch/taillard/problemes.dir/ordonnancement.dir/ordonnancement.html).

24. **Resource-constrained project scheduling.** Gate: all precedences and renewable/non-renewable resource limits hold at every time; recalculate makespan and gap on PSPLIB, including multi-mode variants only in their declared lane. Economic guardrail: value earlier completion against compute and schedule churn; prohibit gains obtained through undeclared extra resources. Source: [PSPLIB](https://www.om-db.wi.tum.de/psplib/).

25. **Hospital nurse rostering.** Gate: zero hard-constraint violations for coverage, qualifications, contracts and rest; recalculate weighted soft penalties, runtime and stability on held-out instances. Economic guardrail: report overtime/agency cost and changed assignments, while imposing non-tradeable safe coverage and fatigue limits. Source: [Nurse Rostering Benchmark Instances](https://www.schedulingbenchmarks.org/nrp/).

26. **Examination and course timetabling.** Gate: zero room, student, lecturer or curriculum conflicts; independently score capacity, spacing, compactness and stability penalties under the frozen ITC definition. Economic guardrail: reduce rooms, disruption and administrative time without degrading accessibility or creating impractical late schedules. Source: [International Timetabling Competition 2007](https://www.unitime.org/itc2007/).

## Communications and sensing — 10

27. **Forward-error-correction decoder.** Gate: reproduce BER/FER curves at frozen code/rate/channel/SNR points and known vectors; report throughput, latency, RAM and joules/decoded bit against AFF3CT anchors. Economic guardrail: improved reliability or throughput must justify silicon area, power and implementation complexity. Source: [AFF3CT](https://aff3ct.github.io/index.html).

28. **5G uplink channel estimation and receiver.** Gate: use frozen 5G NR PUSCH and ray-traced/stochastic channel configurations; report BLER/BER, spectral efficiency, throughput, latency, compute and power, with standards-conformant transmitted data. Economic guardrail: gains must fit UE/base-station real-time and power budgets without excessive pilot overhead or specialist hardware. Source: [Sionna 5G NR link-level simulations](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v1.2.2/phy/tutorials/Link_Level_Simulations_with_RT.html).

29. **Adaptive-bitrate video streaming.** Gate: chronological replay and then randomized live trials where possible; measure delivered quality, rebuffer time, startup delay, quality switches, latency and bytes, with confidence intervals because network outcomes are heavy-tailed. Economic guardrail: CDN/compute cost per viewing hour and abandonment must both improve. Source: [Stanford Puffer data and experiments](https://puffer.stanford.edu/data-description/).

30. **Real-time speech denoising and dereverberation.** Gate: frozen DNS development plus hidden clips; measure standardized SIG, BAK and overall quality, word accuracy, causal latency and real-time factor. Economic guardrail: report cloud/device cost, bandwidth and battery per audio hour; non-causal or oversized models cannot enter the real-time lane. Source: [Microsoft DNS Challenge](https://github.com/microsoft/DNS-Challenge).

31. **Acoustic machine-fault detection.** Gate: evaluate blind machine types and domains using AUC, pAUC, precision, recall and F1; measure false alarms, inference latency, model size and energy. Economic guardrail: combine missed-failure and false-maintenance costs, and require deployment cost below the expected avoided downtime. Source: [DCASE 2025 Task 2](https://dcase.community/challenge2025/task-first-shot-unsupervised-anomalous-sound-detection-for-machine-condition-monitoring).

32. **Smartphone sensor denoising.** Gate: use untouched SIDD benchmark pairs; report raw and sRGB PSNR/SSIM, colour error, latency, RAM, model size and energy, with no test-image adaptation. Economic guardrail: meet camera-pipeline frame deadlines and mobile power/memory limits; server-sized improvements remain in an exploratory lane. Source: [SIDD](https://abdokamel.github.io/sidd/index.html).

33. **Efficient image super-resolution.** Gate: first meet the frozen DIV2K/LSDIR PSNR threshold, then minimize runtime, parameters, FLOPs, RAM and energy on fixed hardware; verify output dimensions and prohibit hidden-test training. Economic guardrail: must meet a declared phone, TV or edge latency/power profile rather than win through model size. Source: [DIV2K](https://data.vision.ee.ethz.ch/cvl/DIV2K/).

34. **Road-scene optical flow.** Gate: KITTI’s hidden evaluator recomputes endpoint error and outlier rates; also report runtime, failure rate, RAM and energy across the complete set. Economic guardrail: meet the sensor frame deadline on priced hardware; accuracy gains that require an uneconomic GPU or miss real-time operation are separate Pareto points. Source: [KITTI optical-flow benchmark](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/eval_stereo_flow.php?benchmark=flow).

35. **Visual–inertial navigation.** Gate: run EuRoC sequences from raw camera/IMU inputs without future leakage; align trajectories by the declared convention and report translation/rotation error, drift, tracking failures, latency and power. Economic guardrail: improve positioning per watt and hardware pound/dollar while sustaining real-time operation; external map/services must be costed. Source: [EuRoC MAV dataset](https://projects.asl.ethz.ch/datasets/euroc-mav/).

36. **Camera–radar–LiDAR 3D perception.** Gate: use the official nuScenes split and evaluator; report NDS/mAP plus per-class, distance, weather and lighting results, latency, RAM and energy. Economic guardrail: state sensor and compute BOM, watts and frame deadline; promotion requires minimum safety-critical subgroup performance, not only a higher average. Source: [nuScenes](https://www.nuscenes.org/).

## Software, compute and databases — 11

37. **General compiler optimisation.** Gate: every LLVM test-suite reference output must match; measure runtime, compile time, code size and energy across the full frozen suite, with a licensed SPEC CPU 2026 audit for major claims. Economic guardrail: compute-hour/energy savings must exceed added compilation time and binary footprint. Sources: [LLVM test-suite guide](https://llvm.org/docs/TestSuiteGuide.html) and [SPEC CPU 2026](https://www.spec.org/cpu2026/).

38. **Embedded compiler optimisation.** Gate: exact Embench outputs on declared MCU/ISA, with repeated measurements of speed, flash bytes, RAM and joules; inspect generated code and run hardware tests, not simulator-only claims. Economic guardrail: translate improvements into flash/RAM BOM, battery life and build-time cost. Source: [Embench](https://www.embench.org/).

39. **Transactional database engine.** Gate: full TPC-C transactional, consistency, durability and response-time rules; report tpmC, p95/p99 latency, recovery, energy and price/performance on fully disclosed hardware/software. Economic guardrail: total licensed system cost and watts per sustained transaction count; no performance gained by weakening ACID. Source: [TPC current specifications](https://tpc.org/TPC_Documents_Current_Versions/current_specifications5.asp).

40. **Ad-hoc analytical SQL engine.** Gate: exact TPC-H answers, valid data generation, load/refresh and power/throughput tests at declared scale; report QphH, latency distribution, RAM, storage and energy. Economic guardrail: dollars per QphH including hardware, licensing, storage and cloud egress. Source: [TPC-H current specification and tools](https://tpc.org/TPC_Documents_Current_Versions/current_specifications5.asp).

41. **Complex decision-support database.** Gate: exact TPC-DS outputs and all prescribed query/maintenance phases at the chosen scale; report QphDS, failed/time-out queries, tail latency, load time, storage and energy. Economic guardrail: total cost per completed query mix, not a cherry-picked fast subset. Source: [TPC-DS current specification and tools](https://tpc.org/TPC_Documents_Current_Versions/current_specifications5.asp).

42. **Key-value and cloud-serving store.** Gate: YCSB workloads A–F with verified operation results, declared consistency and crash recovery; report throughput, p50/p95/p99/p99.9 latency, storage/write amplification, RAM and energy. Economic guardrail: dollars and joules per million durable operations at the required tail-latency SLA. Source: [YCSB](https://github.com/brianfrankcooper/YCSB).

43. **Streaming-data execution engine.** Gate: Nexmark output cardinalities and values must match for all selected queries, including windows, late data and recovery; measure events/s, event-time tail latency, checkpoint/restart time and energy. Economic guardrail: cloud/hardware cost per million correctly processed events at the declared SLA. Source: [Apache Beam Nexmark](https://beam.apache.org/documentation/sdks/java/testing/nexmark/).

44. **Large-scale graph traversal.** Gate: Graph500 validation must pass for graph construction and BFS/SSSP outputs at declared scale; report TEPS, runtime, memory, network traffic and Green Graph500 energy. Economic guardrail: TEPS per watt and per total system cost, including interconnect and storage rather than CPU alone. Source: [Graph500 rules](https://graph500.org/?page_id=457).

45. **Sparse iterative scientific solver.** Gate: official HPCG validation and residual checks pass for the required run length; report HPCG FLOP/s, time, memory, scaling and energy with all substitutions disclosed. Economic guardrail: validated solves per kWh and per system cost, including interconnect and memory capacity. Source: [HPCG](https://hpcg-benchmark.org/).

46. **Sparse-matrix kernels and factorisation.** Gate: frozen stratified matrices from real application groups; verify SpMV/factor/solve results against high-precision references and report preprocessing, execution, RAM, bandwidth and energy. Economic guardrail: amortize setup over the declared repeat count and price required hardware; do not hide expensive format conversion. Source: [SuiteSparse Matrix Collection](https://sparse.tamu.edu/).

47. **External and distributed sorting.** Gate: generate input and validate sorted output under official GraySort rules; report TB/min, total elapsed time, storage/network traffic, faults/restarts and energy. Economic guardrail: dollars and joules per correctly sorted TB including all machines and temporary storage. Source: [Sort Benchmark](https://sortbenchmark.org/).

This set deliberately keeps “better” multidimensional: correctness first, then a verified Pareto improvement in useful output, time, memory, energy or money.
